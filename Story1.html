The Girl and the Eggs
Part 1

It was going to be a crisp winter day, one where you could see your breath, but still only need a scarf to stay warm.
The station was empty. Occasionally, flashes of dark metal rushed by from out of the mountain back into it. Never stopping and pausing. If they did they would notice one customer that was waiting patiently. Holding big round piles of snow in her arms. As if she was ready to have a snowball fight with whoever stopped.
The only employee in the station had been watching her for the better part of 10 minutes. She was impeccably still, only sometimes shifting her piles of snow. How she wasn't shivering he wouldn't know. Only wearing a thin long red coat and a scarf to match that waved in the blowing wind of the speeding trains.
He had already called out to her. Two times. The first when he wanted her attention to make her buy a ticket, only to be ignored. And the second time, just now and he had earned a side-eyed in return. What a strange girl, he thought, shaking his head and turned back to his newspaper. Not a lot of news reached this far out, but some big stuff did. Like horses getting out of a rich man's stable, a new singer going missing after their performance, fake dragon eggs at a royal palace.
Speaking of dragons, the employee drifted to the sculpture that stood in the middle of his station. A sleeping dragon covered in the snow, its dark stone scales shining in the breaking daylight. Whatever kept rich people entertained in extinct species, at least gave him artwork to admire.
Finally, a train stopped. It only had one passenger, a conductor to trade his post. The conductor left his train, not even noticing the strange girl who had gotten closer and joined the original station employee. They started to chat, catching up to the present, but all she had borrowed was telepathic memory, not actual telepathy. And she didn't need to know about their lives.
The girl shifted her armfuls once more. The snow was melting into her clothes, making her shiver. She needed to leave while the station employees were distracted with one another. — She walked around the train, thankfully it had no passenger boxes. Making it easy to slip behind and find the door to the inside and struggled to open it with one hand. She took the loop in her scarf and dropped her cargo into the soft nest. She grabbed the door handle firmly and yanked as hard as she could. The door slipped open, and she wasted no time in throwing herself inside, climbing up the large steps that would have been easy for the longed-legged conductors. Just as she climbed the final step, something scraped the back of her leg.
"GET OUTTA OF THERE!" He bellowed. His voice was deep, rattling inside her head as she shrieked. The girl was dragged back down by her leg. She scraped her forearms and elbows as she tried to anchor herself to the ground. She scrambled to push her scarf up off her neck, not wanting to crush her bundle as she flailed wildly to get the employee off of her. kicking with her legs to get distance between them. The entrance was only big enough for one of the employees to grab her and she could see the other one standing behind the other ready to help.
She shrieked again and kicked out harder. "Get off!" Landing one good blow into his sternum, he coughed and doubled over. The other employee made a horrified sound and grabbed him. The girl pulled her legs into the cart, grabbed the handle and slammed the door shut, twisting it as far as it could go to lock it. Just as she did, thumping on the other side started.
She sat on those steps and breathed heavily, heart beating wildly. The conductor's cart was strange, dials and winding wheels. She grabbed a handle and pulled herself up, only to slip as it pulled down to her. At the same time the train lurched, making her slip straight into the wheel.
The voices outside became more frantic. She couldn't pick out words, but she could hear the sound of a radio "Stop her at-" and she really didn't want to know more. Gritting her teeth, she lifted the handle to bring it up and around, bracing herself for the sudden stop and lurch. The train started to move speeding faster as it pitched downward into the ground. It open up an underground space full of tracks, it was dimly lit, bright red flares at the base of the tracks. She didn't have time to admire craftmanship of such a big space because just after a few minutes of warm lighting it started to blare red paired with a loud drone. They knew she was down here and apparently had protocols for it. The wheels of the train screeching as they stopped. Her wheel locking in place at the same time. A sitting duck.
The girl couldn't stay like this and gathered up her scarf and slid outside, running into the open. She could hear whistles of other trains and people shouting. Followed her track, she only just made it to another train entrance before a crack of a thousand trees snapping exploded the wall in front of her into dust. She ducked and squeezed her eyes as more came down. Shouts from the armed guards came from behind her, like thunder before lighting. She ran down the tunnel to get away.
It was a maze. Tracks going in and out of walls, some were closed and others weren't. She slipped on gravel and ran blind. She turned a corner, hoping to lose them in their own system. And ran straight into a wall. A small, living wall.
She stumbled backwards, turning her body to shield the eggs from grabbing hands that never came. She had squeezed her eyes shut when she braced for a fight, so when she opened them she was surprised to find a young boy. No older than 13 maybe 12. He was dirty, covered in black dust and clothes that had tiny rips in them. And he was glaring at her.
"What's your problem!" Getting up and brushing off rocks that had stuck to his pants. He grumbled more, but the girl wasn't paying attention to him anymore. She had fallen in their collide and had dropped one side of her bundle. She trembled as she traced the hairline crack in one the eggs, it was silver against the dark shell. But she didn't have time to mourn injuries. The stampede of guards were closing in.
She looked around, most of the tunnels in this part were open, she could go down any one of them. But, she would still be running. Endangering the eggs more. And who knew how long she could keep going while wanted. Did she want to keep running? Behind the boy one of the tunnels was barely open, you would have to army crawl to get underneath. But even if she did hide there, could she trust the boy to not say anything? Or…
She snapped her head to the boy, finally tuning into what he was saying. "-honestly lady, did you fall off your train or something? At least this area is deact-"
"Can you keep a secret?" The boy looked at her with his mouth agape. Offended in her interrupting him and confused at her question.
"What?"
She got up, haphazardly. Pulling her scarf off the eggs and basically shoving them at him.
"Can. You. Keep. A. Secret?" He stared down at the eggs, still gaping, but his eyes seemed to bug out of his head now. His eyes darting from her to the eggs, to possibly looking for a trusted adult, then back to the eggs. He sputtered and let out choking tiny laughs.
"I- um ha! That s-seems to be b-be two serec- Secrets!" His hands made aborted attempts to push them back towards her, but she was already leading him backwards.
When they got close to the half-closed gate she shoved them fully into his arms, the scarf falling to the ground. She put her hands on his shoulders and she pushed him down and under the gate. While he still sputtered and tried to deny them.
"Wait- no- my dad!" "Wait here for me, don't get caught. If I don't find you meet me at the Merchant's center. The only bakery." He shut his mouth with a clack when he tried again to speak, but her glare wasn't worth it. She gathered her scarf in her arms as she repeated her instructions, this time with a lucrative deal to keep him quiet.
"The Merchant's center, bakery. If you meet me there I'll give you a reward. Whatever you want." That made the boy perk up and shrink back down. She didn't have time to hear his answer. Only to hope he listened. The guards got closer. Another shot that rang out from an overconfident shooter. But it was enough for them to catch her tail.
She ran, not looking back. Trying to keep her scarf in a bunched up pile. She wanted to lose them, but she couldn't afford for them to continue looking for her down here. She played a cold chilling game of tag, weaving between corners and into hopefully not running trains. All while dodge any stray bullets, they risked to shoot. Gravel turned into cut-stone, train system into what looked like a storage area. She now had to dodge boxes and stray boards on the ground. At least there was sun light now. Beams that opened up to the outside world.
She passed one before stopping, walking back to that window of light. She looked down, water. On the other side of the mountain was a large lake that was a good 50 foot drop from where she stood. She could hear the guards getting closer. And she was tired of running. She ran back towards the battalion, stopping right before them.
The guards stop charging when they see her just standing there. The bundle in her arms. The captain, the only one covered in gleaming, embellished metal, threw out his arm to stop his men from shooting. He stepped cautiously towards her, speaking gently.
"Give me the eggs." She looked wide-eyed at the man. She didn't say anything to him, just clutching her pile just a bit tighter. He narrowed his eyes, clearly patience run thin. He must have not liked running in that armor.
"Give me the eggs!" He barked now, gritting his teeth. She took a step back, taunting. But then.. she braced her arms. And threw the scarf right at them.
The men scrambled to catch whatever fell out of the scarfs protective wrap, but it just landed in a heap. Eggs gone. And when they looked up to the girl, she was gone too. Running away.
The captain yelled in anger, ordering his men to follow her. But when they did finally get close. She turned a corner and… they heard a splash. The men stood silent in front of the corner all of them tense. The captain pushed his way to the front, and when he got to the front, he saw the column of free space just as she had. Water rippling, nothing but bubbles surface every so often in the same spot, but she did not resurface. After a minute or two the bubbles stopped.
The captain huffed in frustration. "Go down to the lake. Find her body." A couple of the men looked at each other in apprehension. The captain barked at them " GO!" And the men scurried away. Clearly fearing him more than searching a dark lake.
The captain stayed at the ledge for a bit longer. He had the scarf, it clenched tightly in his gloved fist. He scoffed, letting go of it. It waved in the wind, floating down to where its owner fell.
"Stupid girl.."
He marched back the way they had come. Already dreading the report he was going to have to write. And the eggs. Nascatha, he needed to find the eggs. Where ever she had stashed them.
---
The boy stayed in the dark tunnel for a long time. He didn't know whether he was paralyzed because he was holding eggs bigger than his head, or because he had never heard gun shots that close before.
"Linek?! Linek where are you?" A voice calls out past the gate. Only one person knew his name down here, but he was still hesitant before he started to wiggle his way out of the tunnel. Some distance away he saw the large from of his father and almost cried in relief. Almost. But he did cry out for his father.
"Dad!" His dad gave a deep sigh in relief, his soot covered hands reaching out to pull the boy from underneath fully.
"What- were you doing.. In there..?" His dad slowly pulled his son to hang in front of him. The two large eggs nestled in his son's arms. But before the boy could open his mouth, he was interrupted once more. A soft crackling sound as one of eggs seemed to rock in his arms, nocking into the other one and setting it off too.
The father and son could only look at each other as the eggs hatched.
---
The bag of beans had been perfectly positioned so that she could hop into the barrel, push them off the lid and put it on her head, before the guards were any wiser. And when finally the last man left, she pushed the lid off carefully. She stayed quiet as she sneaked down the opposite way he went. Hiding behind boxes and shelves anytime she heard someone coming. When she finally found a way out, a metal vent that had sunlight filtering through, she climbed into it. And when she fell out of it at the same time, she landed into a hay pile. On the other side of the wall was a pasture. And she had disturbed the cows here dinner. And they made their disapproval apparent loudly.
